# 📐 Symbolic Collapse Model – Mathematical Appendix

## 𝒞: Collapse Pressure Formula

The core metric for symbolic system strain is defined as:

$$ \mathcal{C} = \frac{I \cdot S \cdot P}{T \cdot E} $$

Where:

* $I$ = Information Load
* $S$ = Symbolic Abstraction
* $P$ = Polarization
* $T$ = Transmission Fidelity
* $E$ = Epistemic Coherence

This models how overload and fragmentation ($I, S, P$) interact with stabilizing forces ($T, E$) in communication and reasoning systems.

## 🧠 Risk Thresholds

These qualitative thresholds interpret symbolic collapse pressure:

| Collapse Pressure (𝒞) | Risk Zone          |
| :-------------------- | :----------------- |
| 𝒞 < 1.5               | ✅ Stable          |
| 1.5 ≤ 𝒞 < 2.5         | ⚠️ Symbolic Stress |
| 2.5 ≤ 𝒞 < 3.5         | 🚧 Narrative Fracture |
| 𝒞 ≥ 3.5               | 🔥 Collapse Zone   |

Use these zones to annotate simulation output or UI feedback.

## 🧮 Optional Extensions

### 1. Weighted Sensitivity (Power-Law Modulation)

Some systems may be more sensitive to certain factors. Use exponents to scale their impact:

$$ \mathcal{C}_{weighted} = \frac{I^\alpha \cdot S^\beta \cdot P^\gamma}{T^\delta \cdot E^\epsilon} $$

Example weights:

* $\alpha = 1.2$ (Information load is volatile)
* $\epsilon = 1.5$ (Epistemic coherence is stabilizing)

### 2. Collapse Acceleration (Rate of Change)

To track how fast collapse is progressing, calculate:

$$ \frac{d\mathcal{C}}{dt} $$

Used to identify critical transitions or tipping points.

### 3. Symbolic Entropy Proxy

Inspired by Shannon entropy, this models uncertainty in shared meaning:

$$ H_{symbol} = -\sum p(x) \log p(x) $$

Where $p(x)$ is the distribution of interpretations of a symbol.

As entropy increases, so does $\mathcal{C}$. High entropy implies symbolic fragmentation.

### 4. Epistemic Recovery

Systems with high $T$ or $E$ may dampen collapse over time:

$$ \mathcal{C}_{t+1} = \mathcal{C}_t \cdot (1 - r) \quad \text{if } T,E > \text{threshold} $$

Where $r$ is the resilience factor (e.g., 10–30%).
